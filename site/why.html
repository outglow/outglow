Updates from your Zigbee hubNoYes

The original firmware has no Zigbee update support; the only way to update it is the vendor app, over Bluetooth, next to the bulb. With Outglow, new versions show up in Zigbee2MQTT like for any other device.

Switches off at the end of a fadeNoYes

A hub's “fade to off” dims to the lowest level and expects the light to switch off there, as the Zigbee Cluster Library says. The original firmware stays at its dimmest glow instead. Outglow follows revision 8 of the library and was checked against its text.

Recovers when a router disappearsPoorlyYes

Bulbs are the routers of your Zigbee network, so this matters for every device behind them. With both firmwares in one emulated network, an original bulb kept sending to a router that was gone for as long as we watched (four minutes), and after a short power cut it sometimes searched every channel and never came back. Outglow drops a router it no longer hears, finds a new route and rejoins after power cuts.

Network diagnostics in your hubNoYes

Outglow shows how many frames it relayed, retried and lost, its neighbours and children, why it last restarted, and how much radio time Bluetooth took: all 32 counters of the Zigbee Diagnostics cluster, plus a few of its own. More below.

The bulb has one radio. Time spent on Bluetooth is time it can't hear Zigbee. Outglow lets you keep Bluetooth on, switch it off, or keep it only for a few minutes after the bulb is powered on, from your hub. A reset turns it back on. How to set it.

The tests in numbers

Each test takes one requirement from the text of a standard and checks it on the running firmware: the real firmware images, the original ones included, run in the Outglow emulator with a scripted coordinator or phone on the other side. A test passes when the bulb does what the clause says. The tests and the emulator are in the source; this page is built from their results each time the website is built.

What you gain, what you lose

Gains

  • Updates from your hub. Outglow is an OTA Upgrade client: Zigbee2MQTT offers new versions like for any other device. The original firmware has no Zigbee OTA client and is updated only by the vendor app, next to the bulb.
  • A router that follows the network layer. It drops neighbours it no longer hears, reports routes it cannot deliver on, repeats broadcasts its neighbours missed, notices address conflicts and handles a PAN identifier change as R22 says. Details under Zigbee.
  • Diagnostics. The Diagnostics cluster with all 32 standard counters, its own counters, and readable values for Zigbee2MQTT without any converter. Below.
  • Settings. Bluetooth on, off or only for a while after power-on, and whether end devices may join through the bulb. Below.
  • Faster joining. It scans the primary Zigbee channels first, as BDB 3.0.1 says, and is in the network in about 1.5 s instead of about 5 s.
  • Lights that do what the cluster library says. “With On/Off” fades end switched off, colour temperature moves stop where asked, scenes keep what they were given and take their transition time, groups and scenes answer the queries a hub broadcasts, unknown or broken commands are refused instead of carried out.
  • Standard Bluetooth answers. Error responses where the standard asks for them, so a phone that probes the bulb gets an answer instead of waiting 30 s for a timeout; the vendor app works as before.
  • Open source, under the GPL, with the emulator and the tests that produced this page.

Losses and trade-offs

  • One radio for two networks. Both firmwares share one radio between Zigbee and Bluetooth. Outglow keeps Zigbee first: in a busy network it may skip a Bluetooth advertising event (at most three in a row) and, while Zigbee neighbours were heard in the last 64 ms, advertise on one Bluetooth channel instead of three. The app and the flasher may then take a little longer to find the bulb. A single Zigbee broadcast that arrives during an advertising event can still be missed; hubs repeat broadcasts, and switching Bluetooth off (Settings) removes it.
  • A short wait in the main loop. With nothing to do, Outglow's main loop waits up to 1 ms. A received frame ends the wait at once; timers of the stack and of the light are taken up at most 1 ms later.
  • A new model in your hub. Outglow reports its own model (ManufacturerName “Outglow”), so Zigbee2MQTT must interview the bulb again after the switch, and entity names in Home Assistant may change. Without the converter, the generated definition has no start-up brightness setting.
  • Bluetooth notifications ignore the descriptor. The vendor app switches notifications on with its own command, as the original firmware expects, so Outglow sends them even while the standard descriptor (CCCD) is off. One Bluetooth test fails on Outglow for that reason, as it does on the original firmware.
  • Little hardware time. Outglow runs on a few of its developers' bulbs so far; most of what this page shows was measured in the emulator.

Settings Outglow adds

Both are written from your hub, kept in the bulb's flash, and come from the public code (objects.rs, outglow.mjs). The original firmware has neither.

When Bluetooth runs

The bulb's radio is shared: every moment it spends on Bluetooth (advertising for the app, a connection, an update) is a moment it cannot hear Zigbee. If you use only your hub, switch Bluetooth off for the steadiest Zigbee; if you want the vendor app or Bluetooth updates now and then, keep it on for a few minutes after each power-on.

  • Three modes in the manufacturer cluster 0xFC5F (manufacturer code 0x1141, endpoint 1): attribute 0x0000 mode (0 always, 1 for a window after power-on, 2 off), 0x0001 the window in minutes (up to 1440), 0x0002 whether Bluetooth is running now.
  • The same setting on endpoint 8, an Analog Output “BLE window after power-on” in minutes: 0 means always on, N means on for N minutes after power-on. (“Off” can only be set in 0xFC5F; endpoint 8 then reads 0 and says it is out of service.)
  • Ways back: switching the power on opens the window again; leaving the network, the power-cycle factory reset and the Zigbee Reset to Factory Defaults command turn Bluetooth back to always on, so a bulb that is in no network can always be reached by the app or the flasher. A Bluetooth connection or update in progress is finished first.

In Zigbee2MQTT without a converter: the entity analog_output_8 (minutes, 0 = always). With the converter: the device's specific settings bluetooth (always, window_after_power_on, off) and bluetooth_window (minutes); the converter writes them again whenever the bulb announces itself, so a reset does not undo your choice for long.

Whether end devices may join through the bulb

End devices are the battery sensors, buttons and remotes that are not routers. Each one picks a parent router and sends everything through it. A mains bulb that is switched off at the wall takes its children offline until they notice and find another parent. If a bulb is often switched off, keep end devices away from it.

  • Endpoint 7, a Binary Output “Allow end devices to join”, on by default. Off: the bulb's beacon offers no room for end devices and a new end device's association or rejoin is refused; children it already has stay; routers still join through it.
  • Kept in flash; back to on after the bulb leaves the network, like the rest of its configuration.

In Zigbee2MQTT, with or without the converter: the switch binary_output_7.

Diagnostics

Outglow counts what it does as a router while it works: frames sent, retried, relayed and lost, neighbours, children, restarts, the radio time Bluetooth took. The counters live in RAM and only grow (wrapping, as the standard expects). Every second they are copied to RAM that a watchdog or software restart leaves alone, so such a restart loses at most a second of counting, and you can see what led to it; a snapshot goes to flash half an hour after the start and then every hour. A power cut loses what was counted since the last snapshot; NumberOfResets is always exact. A factory reset clears them, and so does a manufacturer command (0x00, code 0x1141, in the Diagnostics cluster).

  • The Diagnostics cluster 0x0B05 on endpoint 1, with all 32 attributes of ZCL 8 §3.15 (Tables 3-130 and 3-131), each reportable.
  • Manufacturer attributes from 0x5000 in the same cluster, read with manufacturer code 0x1141: uptime, the reason of the last start, relay losses by cause, queue peaks, Bluetooth time; beyond 0x5014 also signal-strength histograms, the noise floor, the way to the coordinator, the neighbour table and an event log.
  • Endpoints 2–6, Analog Inputs that describe themselves (“Relayed frames lost”, “MAC retries per APS message”, “Radio time lent to BLE”, “Router neighbours”, “End-device children”), so a hub shows them without knowing anything about Outglow. Endpoints 7 and 8 are the settings.
  • Over Bluetooth, the browser console reads the same 32 + 21 counters from the bulb itself, from the same values the cluster answers with.

How to read them

  • Zigbee2MQTT without a converter: the generated entities analog_in_count_2 (relayed frames lost), analog_input_3 (MAC retries per message), analog_in_percentage_4 (radio time lent to Bluetooth), analog_in_count_5 (router neighbours) and analog_in_count_6 (end-device children). They are reported on change; the Refresh button next to an entity reads it at once.
  • With the converter (z2m/outglow.mjs): the device option diagnostics picks summary (default: link_health, coordinator_route, neighbors, uptime) or full (every counter, in groups, and the neighbour table and event log), polled every diagnostics_poll_interval seconds (600 by default); diagnostics_refresh reads everything now and diagnostics_clear clears the counters.
  • By hand, in Zigbee2MQTT's Dev console: endpoint 1, cluster haDiagnostic, an attribute such as macTxUcastRetry; for a manufacturer attribute give its number (0x5007) and manufacturer code 0x1141.
  • Without a hub: the console connects to a bulb over Bluetooth from Chrome or Edge and explains every counter.
The fields, and what a healthy value looks like

Presence detection experimental

A person between two bulbs changes how strongly each hears the other. With presence detection built in, every bulb sends a short probe frame a few times a second (5 by default) and measures the signal strength (RSSI) of the probes of its neighbours. Each sender heard is a line; when a line's signal starts to swing more than its usual noise, someone is crossing it, and the bulb reports occupancy. Philips Hue's MotionAware works on the same idea.

  • The probes go to a separate PAN identifier (0x5E55), without acknowledgement: devices without the feature drop them in their radio filter, nobody relays them. About 1 ms of air time each, roughly 0.5 % of the channel per bulb.
  • In Zigbee: the Occupancy Sensing cluster 0x0406 (occupancy, and how long until it goes back to unoccupied) and the manufacturer cluster 0xFC5E (code 0x1141): the sensitivity, probes per second, calibration, self-tuning and automatic calibration. Reports go to the coordinator on every change and every half hour.
  • Sensitivity (0–100, 40 by default; 0 switches detection off) is kept per line. Out of the box each line calibrates itself: during its first full day the bulb finds the quietest hour of every line and takes the most sensitive setting that hour left quiet. A calibration by hand records an empty room for some seconds instead; setting the sensitivity by hand switches the automatic calibration off. An optional daily self-tuning follows slow changes of a line's noise.
  • In Zigbee2MQTT with the converter: occupancy, occupancy_timeout, motion_sensitivity, motion_probes, motion_auto_calibration, motion_self_tuning, motion_calibrate (start or stop a recording of the empty room) and motion_calibration_left.
Experimental, and not in the images you install Presence detection is built only on request (cargo xtask build --sense) and has not run on a bulb. It was measured in the emulator, on a channel model built from the link levels and spread the bulbs' own neighbour tables reported; how much a person really changes those links, and whether a day is enough to calibrate a real room, is not known yet. It needs at least three bulbs around a room, and a line through a wall sees both rooms.

Zigbee, behaviour by behaviour

Each part says what the behaviour is for, where the standard defines it, and what each firmware did in the tests. The line under it counts the tests; open the list to see each test, the clause it checks and, where a firmware did something else, what it did. On the tested original version: ….

Link status: who hears whom

Every 15 s a router tells its neighbours how well it hears each of them. Routes are chosen from those link costs, and a neighbour that falls silent for three periods must be dropped, or traffic keeps going to a router that is gone.

Zigbee R22 §3.6.3.4

  • Original firmware Sends link status and sets the costs right, but keeps reporting a neighbour that went silent more than six periods ago as reachable.
  • Outglow Ages silent neighbours out after three periods.

Broadcasts

A broadcast (a group command, a route request, an announcement) travels by every router passing it on once. A router repeats it while its neighbours have not been heard passing it on, and recognises copies so the application sees each broadcast only once.

Zigbee R22 §3.6.5

  • Original firmware Passes a broadcast on once and never repeats it, even when no neighbour relayed it. A copy that arrives a few seconds later reaches the application again, so a group command can run twice.
  • Outglow Repeats as the standard says and drops copies.

Routing

Routers find routes by route requests and replies, relay frames hop by hop, keep routes to the coordinator (many-to-one) and follow source routes the coordinator chooses.

Zigbee R22 §3.6.3

  • Original firmware Relays route discovery, many-to-one and source-routed frames correctly. When it looks for a route itself it sends the request more often than the standard allows.
  • Outglow Does all of it as specified.

Telling the sender when delivery fails

When a router cannot pass a frame on, it sends the source a Network Status, so the source can look for another route instead of waiting in vain.

Zigbee R22 §3.6.3.3, §3.6.3.7, §3.6.13

  • Original firmware Says nothing when it has no route for a frame it should relay. Does not answer unknown network commands (that came with R22).
  • Outglow Reports both.

Address conflicts, PAN and channel changes

Two devices can end up with the same short address; routers must notice and report it so one of them moves. A coordinator can also move the whole network to a new PAN identifier or channel, and ask a router to scan the channels' energy.

Zigbee R22 §3.6.1.9, §3.6.9, §2.4.3.3.9

  • Original firmware Does not notice an address conflict. On a PAN identifier change it switches at once and passes the change on under the new identifier, which neighbours still on the old one do not hear. Channel changes and energy scans work.
  • Outglow Reports conflicts, waits before switching the PAN identifier, changes channel and scans.

Leaving, removal and binding

Who may make a bulb leave the network, remove a device, reset the bulb or change its bindings, and which requests a bulb must ignore.

Zigbee R22 §3.6.1.10, §2.4.3.3; ZCL 8 §3.2.2.3

  • Original firmware Results not published (see above).
  • Outglow Leaves only when it should and ignores the requests it must ignore.

Security and keys

The network key protects every frame; the Trust Center hands it out and replaces it; frame counters stop replays; a device that missed a key change gets back by a Trust Center rejoin.

Zigbee R22 §4; BDB 3.0.1 §10

  • Original firmware Results not published (see above).
  • Outglow Follows chapter 4 of R22 in every test here.

Parent role: sensors and remotes

A router is the parent of the end devices that joined through it: it holds frames for them while they sleep, answers for them, forgets them when they time out, and notices when one has moved to another parent.

Zigbee R22 §3.6.1, §3.6.10, §3.6.3

  • Original firmware Holds frames, answers for its children and ages them out correctly. When a child moved to another router it keeps it, and frames for it keep coming to the old parent.
  • Outglow Does all of it; a child that moved is let go.

Delivery between applications (APS)

The application support layer acknowledges messages, retries the ones not acknowledged, drops duplicates, delivers group messages, and splits long messages into fragments.

Zigbee R22 §2.2

  • Original firmware Acknowledges and delivers groups correctly. A retry that arrives late is carried out a second time. Its own retries are fewer and slower than the standard gives. It does not understand the extended header and has no fragmentation.
  • Outglow Passes them. It asked for no acknowledgement in what the retry tests provoked, so those two could not be judged.

Discovery: descriptors, addresses, tables

How a hub learns what a device is and what it knows: node and simple descriptors, endpoints, addresses, the neighbour and routing tables (the network map in Zigbee2MQTT), permit joining.

Zigbee R22 §2.4

  • Original firmware Answers the descriptor and address requests correctly. It does not answer a routing table request, so a network map shows no routes through it.
  • Outglow Answers all of them.

Joining a network

How a factory-new bulb finds a network and what it does after joining: announce itself, open the network for the next device, take a new link key.

BDB 3.0.1 §8; Zigbee R22 §2.4.3.3.7

  • Original firmware Scans all sixteen channels in order instead of the primary channels first. It opens the network with a broadcast to every device rather than to the routers.
  • Outglow Joins as BDB says.

Reading, writing and reporting attributes

The commands every cluster shares: read and write attributes, discover them, configure reports, and answer a command that cannot be carried out with the right status.

ZCL 8 §2

  • Original firmware Reads, writes and reports correctly in most cases. It carries out commands it should refuse: a command with an unknown manufacturer code, a truncated one. It accepts values outside an attribute's range. Several answers use the older format or status codes, or are missing.
  • Outglow Follows revision 8.

Basic information and Identify

What the bulb says about itself (versions, names, dates), and blinking on request so you can find it.

ZCL 8 §3.2, §3.5; Lighting & Occupancy 1.0 §7.2

  • Original firmware Reports an older cluster library version and lacks some attributes a light must have. Its identify time does not count down. It blinks and answers identify queries.
  • Outglow Has them all.

On/Off and brightness

Switching, timed switching, the global scene, and Level Control: moving, stepping, fading with or without switching, what the bulb does at power-on.

ZCL 8 §3.8, §3.10, §3.19

  • Original firmware On/Off passes every test. A fade “with On/Off” to the minimum leaves it on, at its dimmest glow. It accepts a level the lighting profile does not allow. On the dimmable bulb its output after Off and On is not the one before, at the same level.
  • Outglow Passes them.

Colour temperature

On the tunable white bulb: moving to a colour temperature, moving and stepping, stopping, limits, coupling to brightness, start-up colour temperature.

ZCL 8 §5.2

  • Original firmware A continuous colour temperature move does not stop when told to. Commands it should refuse are answered with success. Coupling to brightness misses the limit. On the dimmable bulb it lists a Color Control cluster without the attributes that cluster must have.
  • Outglow Passes them; the dimmable bulb has no Color Control cluster.

Groups and scenes

Groups let one command switch many bulbs; scenes store and recall a state, with a transition time.

ZCL 8 §3.6, §3.7

  • Original firmware Adds, views and removes groups and scenes. It does not answer the membership queries a hub sends by broadcast. Recalling a scene without a brightness sets the light to its lowest level. A recalled scene jumps instead of taking its transition time. Scenes stay after their groups are removed. Several status codes are wrong or retired.
  • Outglow Passes them.

Diagnostics cluster

The counters a router keeps for the hub to read. Above.

ZCL 8 §3.15

  • Original firmware Does not have the cluster.
  • Outglow Has all of it.

Green Power and updates

A Zigbee 3.0 router passes on the frames of battery-free Green Power switches. An OTA Upgrade client asks the hub for a new image when the hub says one is ready.

Green Power Basic 1.1.2; ZCL 8 §11

  • Original firmware Has the Green Power proxy. It has no OTA Upgrade client.
  • Outglow Has both.

Touchlink has no tests in these suites; Outglow's touchlink target is checked in the emulator against another implementation instead.

Bluetooth, behaviour by behaviour

Over Bluetooth Outglow keeps the original bulb's face (name, services, handles) so the vendor app works; below it, it answers as Bluetooth Core 5.4 says.

Advertising

How the bulb makes itself known to phones: the format and timing of advertising packets, answers to scan and connection requests.

Bluetooth Core 5.4 Vol 6 Part B §2.3, §4.4; Vol 3 Part C §9, §11

  • Original firmware Advertises correctly. It answers requests meant for another device with the same 48 bits but the other address type.
  • Outglow Passes them. Both put the GAP service in the advertised list, which the supplement advises against; the app expects it.

Connections

Keeping a connection: channel hopping, acknowledgements, timing windows, timeouts, parameter and channel map updates at the agreed instant.

Bluetooth Core 5.4 Vol 6 Part B §4.5, §5.1.1, §5.1.2

  • Original firmware Keeps connections well. A connection that never starts is given up only after seconds, not after six events. A parameter update that arrives at or after its instant is mishandled.
  • Outglow Passes them.

Link layer control

Version and feature exchange, unknown requests, ping, data length, PHY, ending a connection, timeouts.

Bluetooth Core 5.4 Vol 6 Part B §2.4.2, §5.1, §5.2

  • Original firmware Sends its version more than once and reports features wrong. A procedure it starts is not timed out.
  • Outglow Passes them; it starts no procedure of its own.

L2CAP

Putting frames together from packets, and the signalling channel's answers.

Bluetooth Core 5.4 Vol 3 Part A §3, §4, §7

  • Original firmware Reassembles frames. It leaves signalling commands it does not know unanswered. It processes a frame whose length does not match.
  • Outglow Passes them.

Attribute protocol (ATT)

Reading and writing the bulb's attributes, and the error answers a phone relies on.

Bluetooth Core 5.4 Vol 3 Part F §3

  • Original firmware Reads and writes. Some requests get no answer at all, so the phone waits for its 30 s timeout. Malformed requests and wrong handles get the wrong error or none. It answers write commands, which must not be answered.
  • Outglow Passes every test that applies; it has no queued writes and asks for no MTU of its own, so those tests do not apply.

Services and characteristics (GATT, GAP)

How a phone discovers the bulb's services, what each characteristic allows, and how notifications are switched on.

Bluetooth Core 5.4 Vol 3 Part G, Vol 3 Part C §12

  • Original firmware Its notifying characteristic has no configuration descriptor. Characteristic declarations read back wrong. The advertised name is not the device name.
  • Outglow One test fails, gatt_cccd_gates_notifications: it notifies even with the descriptor off, because the vendor app switches notifications on its own way (see losses). Two more end in a note (a recommendation, or a clause that can be read two ways) and one could not be judged; the list shows each.

Pairing and encryption

How a bulb without Bluetooth pairing must refuse pairing and encryption requests.

Bluetooth Core 5.4 Vol 3 Part H §2, §3; Vol 6 Part B §5.1.3

  • Original firmware Results not published (see above).
  • Outglow Refuses them as specified.

  • Zigbee Specification R22 (05-3474-22): network, APS, ZDO and security.

  • Base Device Behavior 3.0.1 (16-02828-012): joining and commissioning.
