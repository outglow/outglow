Outglow and the original firmware

What changes when you replace the original firmware with Outglow. “Original” (or “stock”) means the manufacturer’s firmware; Outglow is our replacement. Both run through the same checks in an emulator: a software model of the bulb, with a simulated hub or phone. “Original firmware” on this page refers to the two tested images listed under Versions tested. Results describe those versions and scenarios; they do not establish behaviour for every product or release.

A hub's “fade to off” dims to the lowest level and expects the light to switch off there, as the Zigbee Cluster Library says. The original firmware stays at its dimmest glow instead. Outglow follows revision 8 of the library and was checked against its text.

A router forwards messages for other devices. In the neighbour-timeout check, the original firmware still reported a silent router as reachable after the allowed waiting period. Outglow shows messages forwarded, retried and lost, nearby routers, battery devices connected through it, why it last restarted, and time spent using Bluetooth. It offers the standard Zigbee troubleshooting counters plus extra readings. More below.

Bluetooth can be switched offNoYes, or only for minutes after power-on

The bulb has one radio. Time spent on Bluetooth is time it can't hear Zigbee. Outglow lets you keep Bluetooth on, switch it off, or keep it only for a few minutes after the bulb is powered on, from your hub. A reset turns it back on. How to set it.

Outglow bulbs can notice people moving from how the radio signal between them changes, and report it as occupancy, like a motion sensor. It needs at least two bulbs in the room, and it is off until you switch it on; then the bulbs collect signal readings until they have enough usable samples to calibrate. Motion sensing has been tested in the emulator only, not yet on a real bulb. How it works.

Going back–With a matching original file and a working update connection

The firmware tool can restore a matching original file over Bluetooth. Returning through Zigbee requires an original image packaged for that update path. Outglow leaves the vendor's startup and update program (the bootloader) in place, so reinstalling the original firmware restores its original software. You may need to pair it again.

What you gain, what you lose

Gains

  • Updates from your hub. Outglow can download firmware wirelessly through Zigbee: Zigbee2MQTT offers new versions like for any other device. The original firmware cannot download updates through Zigbee and is normally updated through the vendor app over Bluetooth, near the bulb.
  • More predictable message forwarding. It stops using nearby routers that have gone silent, reports failed deliveries, repeats network-wide messages that neighbours missed, notices duplicate device addresses and lets neighbours hear about a network identifier change before switching. Details under Zigbee.
  • Diagnostics. Standard network counters, extra troubleshooting information, and readings the hub can show without an extra device definition. Below.
  • Settings. Bluetooth on, off or only for a while after power-on, and whether battery sensors and remotes may connect through the bulb. Below.
  • Faster joining. It searches the preferred Zigbee radio channels first. In the emulator it joins in about a second and a half; the original firmware takes about five seconds.
  • More consistent light controls. Fade-to-off commands end with the light fully off, colour temperature moves stop where asked, scenes keep what they were given and take their transition time, groups and scenes answer the queries a hub broadcasts, unknown or broken commands are refused instead of carried out.
  • Standard Bluetooth answers. Error responses where the standard asks for them, so a phone asking an unsupported question gets an explanation instead of waiting for its request to time out; the vendor app works as before.
  • Open source, under the GNU General Public License, which allows sharing and modifying the code, with the emulator and the tests that produced this page.

Losses and trade-offs

  • One radio for two networks. Both firmwares share one radio between Zigbee and Bluetooth. Outglow gives Zigbee priority when the network is busy, which can make the app or flasher take longer to find the bulb over Bluetooth. A Zigbee message can still be missed while the radio is using Bluetooth. Switching Bluetooth off removes that source of missed messages.
  • A new model in your hub. Outglow identifies itself as an Outglow model, so Zigbee2MQTT must rediscover the bulb’s features after the switch, and entity names in Home Assistant may change. Without the converter, the generated definition has no start-up brightness setting.
  • Bluetooth updates use the app's subscription method. A notification is a status update sent to the phone without waiting for it to ask. The vendor app enables these with its own command. Both firmwares send updates without following the standard Bluetooth subscription switch, so both fail that check.
  • Little hardware time. The documented physical test is on a dimmable filament bulb. The broader hardware and protocol comparisons here come from the emulator.

Settings Outglow adds

The original firmware offers neither of these controls. Outglow lets you change them from your hub and remembers your choice after a power cut. In Zigbee2MQTT, a converter is an optional device definition that gives settings friendlier names and exposes more features; installation details.

When Bluetooth runs

The bulb has one radio shared by Zigbee and Bluetooth. You can keep Bluetooth on, turn it off, or allow it only for a chosen number of minutes after power-on. Limiting Bluetooth gives Zigbee more listening time; keeping a power-on window lets you reach the vendor app or flasher when needed.

With the converter, choose the Bluetooth mode and window length in the device settings. Without it, Zigbee2MQTT calls the window control analog_output_8: its value is the number of minutes, and zero means always on. This control cannot select fully off. The converter reapplies your saved choice when the bulb announces itself to the hub.

Powering on reopens a timed window. Leaving the network or resetting the bulb restores always-on Bluetooth. When a timed window ends, an existing Bluetooth connection can continue until it closes. Choosing fully off disconnects an ordinary connection; a firmware update is allowed to finish first.

Whether battery devices may connect through the bulb

Sensors and remotes often sleep to save their batteries. They choose a nearby router to hold messages and forward their traffic; Zigbee calls that router their “parent”. Switching that bulb off at the wall can leave them disconnected until they find another parent.

Outglow lets you stop new battery devices choosing this bulb as a parent. Existing connected devices stay, and other routers may still join through it. Zigbee2MQTT calls the switch binary_output_7, meaning “Allow end devices to join”. It starts enabled and is enabled again when the bulb leaves the network.

Diagnostics

The original firmware does not offer the standard Zigbee troubleshooting counters. Outglow reports messages sent, retried, forwarded and lost; nearby routers; connected battery devices; restarts; and time spent using Bluetooth. These readings help explain why a device is slow or unreachable.

Outglow keeps counters across software restarts, with at most a second of recent counts lost. It also saves periodic copies to permanent storage. A power cut can lose counts since the last copy, although the restart count remains exact. A factory reset or an explicit clear command clears the counters.

  • Without a converter: Zigbee2MQTT shows the main readings with descriptions supplied by the bulb. Its Refresh button reads a value immediately.
  • With the converter: choose summary diagnostics for everyday use, or full diagnostics for all counters, the nearby-device list and the event log. Refresh reads them now; clear resets the counters.
  • Without a hub: the browser console connects over Bluetooth in Chrome or Edge and explains each reading.
What each diagnostic reading means

Presence detection experimental

The original firmware has no motion sensing. Outglow can look for movement by measuring changes in the radio signal between bulbs. Moving across that path can disturb the signal, allowing the bulbs to report occupancy to your hub. It needs at least two bulbs that can hear each other.

Standard builds include the feature, switched off by default. Once enabled, the bulbs send brief measurement messages and collect signal readings for automatic calibration. They need enough usable samples before choosing a setting. You can also calibrate an empty room yourself or adjust sensitivity. The converter exposes the motion-sensing switch, occupancy reading and calibration controls in Zigbee2MQTT. How it works, with an animation.

Measured in the emulator only This feature has not yet run on a real bulb. The tests use a radio model informed by readings from real bulbs, but do not establish how reliably it detects people or calibrates in a real room. A radio path through a wall can respond to movement in either room.

The original firmware’s security and access-control results are not published. Those sections show Outglow’s results only; absence of an original result means neither pass nor fail.

Zigbee, behaviour by behaviour

Each section explains the situation and both firmwares’ observed behaviour. Open the checks for the details and an example of what incorrect handling could cause. These examples explain the rule’s practical purpose; they are possible consequences, not additional measurements or claims about an unpublished result. A standard excerpt is quoted verbatim; the explanation beside it is ours. “Shall” marks a requirement, while “should” marks a recommendation. On the tested original version: ….

Keeping track of nearby routers

We let a neighbouring router go silent and check whether the bulb stops treating it as a usable route. Routers also exchange regular reports about how well they hear one another.

Zigbee R22 §3.6.3.4

  • Original firmware Exchanges connection reports, but still claims a long-silent neighbour is reachable.
  • Outglow Stops using the silent neighbour after the required waiting period.

Messages sent across the network

We send a message meant to spread through the network, called a broadcast. We check whether the bulb retries when neighbours miss it, and whether receiving a repeat can make a light command run twice.

Zigbee R22 §3.6.5

  • Original firmware Passes a network-wide message on once and does not retry when neighbours miss it. A copy arriving a few seconds later can make a group command run twice.
  • Outglow Retries when neighbours have not passed the message on and prevents repeated copies from running the command again.

Finding and following routes

A bulb can forward messages for other devices. We check how it finds a path, sends messages along it, reports the path back to the hub and follows a route chosen by the hub.

Zigbee R22 §3.6.3

  • Original firmware Finds routes to other devices and the hub, and forwards messages along the selected paths. When it looks for a route itself it sends more attempts than allowed.
  • Outglow Finds and follows routes, reports paths to the hub and keeps route-search retries within the required limits in these checks.

Telling the sender when delivery fails

We ask the bulb to forward a message it cannot deliver. It should tell the sender which destination is unreachable, so the sender can look for another path. We also send a network command it does not understand.

Zigbee R22 §3.6.3.3, §3.6.3.7, §3.6.13

  • Original firmware Says nothing when it has no route for a message it should forward. Does not answer the unknown network command used in this test.
  • Outglow Reports failed delivery and explains when a network command is unsupported.

Duplicate addresses and network changes

We create a duplicate device address and ask the network to change its identifier or radio channel. A network identifier distinguishes nearby Zigbee networks. The bulb must pass a change announcement on before switching, so neighbours still hear it. We also ask it to measure channel activity.

Zigbee R22 §3.6.1.9, §3.6.9, §2.4.3.3.9

  • Original firmware Does not notice an address conflict. Switches network identifier before passing the announcement on, so neighbours using the old identifier cannot hear it. Channel changes and radio-activity measurements work.
  • Outglow Reports duplicate addresses, lets neighbours hear about an identifier change before switching, follows channel changes and reports channel activity.

Leaving, resetting and saved device connections

We check which requests may remove or reset a bulb, remove a connected device, or change a saved direct connection between devices. Zigbee calls such a saved connection a binding.

Zigbee R22 §3.6.1.10, §2.4.3.3; ZCL 8 §3.2.2.3

  • Original firmware Results not published (see above).
  • Outglow Handles the tested removal, reset and saved-connection requests as required, and ignores requests that must not be acted on.

Encryption and secure joining

We check how the bulb obtains and replaces encryption keys, rejects old messages sent again, and returns after missing a key change. The hub normally manages these keys; Zigbee calls that security manager the Trust Center.

Zigbee R22 §4; BDB 3.0.1 §10

  • Original firmware Results not published (see above).
  • Outglow Accepts the tested authorised key changes, rejects the tested unprotected or repeated messages, and completes the tested joining and recovery exchanges.

Helping battery sensors and remotes

A battery device chooses a router, called its parent, to forward messages and hold incoming messages while it sleeps. We check delivery, timeouts and whether the bulb notices when a device moves to another parent.

Zigbee R22 §3.6.1, §3.6.10, §3.6.3

  • Original firmware Holds messages for sleeping devices, answers on their behalf and forgets them after their timeout. Keeps claiming a device that has moved to another router, so messages can still be sent the wrong way.
  • Outglow Holds and forwards messages, handles timeouts and stops claiming a device that has moved to another router.

Delivering commands once and handling lost messages

We drop receipt confirmations, resend commands and split long messages into parts. The bulb should recognise repeats, send receipts when requested and put supported long messages back together.

Zigbee R22 §2.2

  • Original firmware Confirms receipt when asked and delivers group messages correctly. A retry that arrives late is carried out a second time. Its own retries are fewer and slower than the standard gives. It ignores ordinary messages with an optional longer heading and cannot reassemble long messages from parts.
  • Outglow Recognises repeats, sends receipts and reassembles long messages. Its outgoing retries remain inconclusive: the test could not make it send a message that requested a receipt.

Letting the hub discover the bulb and its routes

We ask the questions a hub uses to learn what the bulb can do, which nearby devices it hears and which paths it knows. These answers help populate the device controls and network map.

Zigbee R22 §2.4

  • Original firmware Answers the tested device-description and address queries. It leaves route-list queries unanswered, so the hub cannot display its known routes.
  • Outglow Answers the tested capability, address, neighbour and route queries.

Joining a network

We start with an unpaired bulb and check where it searches, how it announces itself and whether it invites further devices to join in the required way.

BDB 3.0.1 §8; Zigbee R22 §2.4.3.3.7

  • Original firmware Searches every radio channel in order instead of trying preferred channels first. It opens the network with a broadcast to every device rather than to the routers.
  • Outglow Searches preferred channels first, announces itself and sends the joining invitation to the hub and routers.

Reading settings and reporting changes

We ask for readings, change settings and configure automatic reports. We also send missing values, invalid settings and unsupported commands to check that the bulb refuses them clearly.

ZCL 8 §2

  • Original firmware Reads, writes and reports correctly in most cases. It carries out commands meant for an unknown manufacturer and commands with missing information. It accepts values outside a setting’s allowed range. Several replies use an older format or outdated error responses, or are missing.
  • Outglow Returns the tested readings and reports in the expected format, honours reporting intervals and rejects the invalid settings and commands used here.

Device information and finding the right bulb

We ask for the model and version information a hub needs. We also ask the light to identify itself visibly, check its remaining time and check that it stops afterwards.

ZCL 8 §3.2, §3.5; Lighting & Occupancy 1.0 §7.2

  • Original firmware Reports an older command-standard version and omits required build-date and product information. Its reported time for identifying the bulb does not count down. It blinks and answers identify queries.
  • Outglow Provides the required information, counts down identification time and visibly identifies the bulb when asked.

Switching and brightness

We switch and dim the light, run timed commands and fades, restore saved brightness and check the chosen behaviour after power returns. A fade-to-off should end fully off; ordinary dimming should keep the light on.

ZCL 8 §3.8, §3.10, §3.19

  • Original firmware Passes the switching checks. A fade-to-off leaves the light on at its dimmest glow. It accepts a brightness value reserved for special use. On the dimmable bulb, switching off and on restores the reported brightness but lowers the signal driving the LEDs in the emulator.
  • Outglow Completes the tested switching and dimming commands, switches fully off after a fade-to-off and restores the expected brightness.

Warmer and cooler white light

On a tunable-white bulb, we change the white tone, stop an ongoing change and test its limits, power-on setting and optional link to brightness. A dimmable-only bulb cannot change white tone.

ZCL 8 §5.2

  • Original firmware When told to stop changing white tone, it jumps to the warmest white instead of staying at the current tone. Commands it should refuse are answered with success. With white tone linked to dimming, full brightness does not quite reach the configured tone. On the dimmable-only bulb it advertises colour controls without the information those controls require.
  • Outglow Moves and stops at the requested white tone, honours the limits and rejects unsupported commands. It does not advertise colour controls on the dimmable-only bulb.

Groups and saved light scenes

A group lets one command control several bulbs. A scene saves light settings for later recall. We check saving, listing, recalling and deleting them, including whether a scene takes the requested time and leaves unspecified settings alone.

ZCL 8 §3.6, §3.7

  • Original firmware Adds, views and removes groups and scenes. It does not answer membership queries sent across the network. Recalling a scene without a brightness sets the light to its lowest level. A recalled scene reaches its target sooner than the requested transition time. Scenes stay after their groups are removed. Several replies give the wrong reason for rejection or use outdated error responses.
  • Outglow Answers the tested membership queries, restores only the saved settings, uses the requested transition time and removes scenes when their groups are removed.

Troubleshooting readings

We check whether the hub can read standard network counters, whether incoming messages and restarts increase the right counters, and whether signal readings reflect the received signal.

ZCL 8 §3.15

  • Original firmware Does not offer these standard troubleshooting readings.
  • Outglow Provides the standard diagnostics service and passes the counter, format and signal-reading checks.

Battery-free switches and wireless updates

We check whether the bulb advertises the standard forwarding service for battery-free Zigbee switches, called Green Power. This checks the service description, not delivery of a switch's commands. Separately, we tell it that firmware is available and check whether it asks the hub for update details.

Green Power Basic 1.1.2; ZCL 8 §11

  • Original firmware Advertises the forwarding service for battery-free switches. It does not request update details in this check; its supported update path uses Bluetooth.
  • Outglow Advertises the switch-forwarding service and requests update details from the hub.

Touchlink, a nearby-controller method of setting up a Zigbee light, is not covered by these suites. Outglow is checked separately against another implementation in the emulator.

Bluetooth, behaviour by behaviour

Outglow preserves the Bluetooth controls expected by the vendor app. These checks examine how a phone discovers the bulb, connects to it, reads values and handles errors.

Being found over Bluetooth

We check the discovery messages that let a phone find the bulb, their timing and replies to connection requests. A Bluetooth address includes both its digits and its category; matching digits alone do not identify the same device.

Bluetooth Core 5.4 Vol 6 Part B §2.3, §4.4; Vol 3 Part C §9, §11

  • Original firmware Advertises correctly. It answers requests with matching address digits even when the address category identifies another device.
  • Outglow Handles the tested discovery and connection requests correctly. Like stock, it advertises a basic Bluetooth housekeeping service that the standard recommends leaving out because the vendor app expects it.

Keeping a Bluetooth connection working

We lose packets, vary timing and change the allowed radio channels. We check whether the bulb stays in step with the phone, recovers from brief silence and gives up a connection within the required deadline.

Bluetooth Core 5.4 Vol 6 Part B §4.5, §5.1.1, §5.1.2

  • Original firmware Keeps connections well. It waits several seconds before abandoning a connection that never starts, beyond the required opening deadline. It loses synchronisation on a timing change received exactly at changeover, and fails to disconnect for one that arrives too late.
  • Outglow Keeps the connection working through the tested losses and valid timing changes, and ends it when the tested timeout or invalid late change requires it.

Agreeing how the Bluetooth connection works

The phone and bulb exchange versions and supported features, agree packet sizes and timing, and reject features they do not support. We check those replies and what happens when an exchange goes unanswered.

Bluetooth Core 5.4 Vol 6 Part B §2.4.2, §5.1, §5.2

  • Original firmware Sends its version more than once. A control request it starts remains unanswered without triggering the required disconnect.
  • Outglow Answers the tested control requests as required. It starts no control exchange of its own in these tests, so checks of those self-initiated exchanges do not apply.

Assembling Bluetooth messages

One message can arrive in several radio packets. We check that the bulb joins them correctly, rejects a wrong message length and answers requests to manage Bluetooth communication channels.

Bluetooth Core 5.4 Vol 3 Part A §3, §4, §7

  • Original firmware Reassembles incoming messages. It leaves unsupported channel-management requests unanswered. It processes a message containing more data than its declared length.
  • Outglow Reassembles the tested messages, discards the length mismatch and answers channel-management requests, including requests it cannot support.

Reading and changing values over Bluetooth

A phone discovers, reads and writes values exposed by the bulb. We check valid requests, invalid value numbers, incomplete messages and agreed reply sizes. Unsupported requests should get a clear answer so the phone does not wait needlessly.

Bluetooth Core 5.4 Vol 3 Part F §3

  • Original firmware Handles ordinary reads and writes. Some requests get no answer at all, so the phone waits for its request timeout. Incomplete requests and invalid value numbers get an incorrect error reply or no reply. It replies to a form of write that must remain unanswered.
  • Outglow Passes the applicable checks. It explicitly declines staged writes, where changes are prepared for later confirmation, and does not initiate message-size negotiation itself.

Discovering Bluetooth features and subscribing to updates

A Bluetooth service groups related functions; a characteristic is a value the phone can read or change. We check their descriptions and permissions, the device name and the standard switch that subscribes the phone to value updates.

Bluetooth Core 5.4 Vol 3 Part G, Vol 3 Part C §12

  • Original firmware It sends updates without offering the standard subscription switch. Reading value descriptions directly gives incomplete information. The discovery name differs from the name read after connection.
  • Outglow Provides readable feature descriptions and a consistent name. It still sends updates when the standard subscription switch is off, following the vendor app’s own command instead. Other qualified or inconclusive results are explained in the checks below.

Bluetooth pairing, encryption and disconnecting

We check how a bulb without standard Bluetooth pairing refuses pairing or encryption requests, describes its support and closes a connection it ends itself. Standard Bluetooth pairing is separate from logging in through the vendor app.

Bluetooth Core 5.4 Vol 3 Part H §2, §3; Vol 6 Part B §5.1.3, §5.1.4, §5.1.6

  • Original firmware Results not published (see above).
  • Outglow Refuses unsupported security setup as required, reports its supported features and follows the tested disconnect procedure.

Test coverage

Each check asks the bulb to do something, or puts it in a particular situation, and observes its reply or simulated LED output. The expected behaviour comes from the Zigbee or Bluetooth standard. A pass means it met that expectation in this test. A failure means it did not; an inconclusive result means the test could not tell. “Not applicable” means the check could not apply to the features or behaviour available in that test. A “note” marks a qualified result, such as a recommendation or a rule with more than one reading. A “test error” means the test itself could not complete. The tests and the emulator are in the source; this page is built from their results each time the website is built.

The standards
