Open firmware for your smart bulbs

Outglow is open-source firmware for EGLO connect.z and AwoX Zigbee bulbs. It turns them into dependable Zigbee routers you can look inside, keeps the Bluetooth app working, and installs from your browser in about five minutes.

What you get

A steadier mesh

Every mains-powered bulb relays traffic for the rest of your network. Outglow picks routes by how many frames actually get through, and does not jump between them at the first lost frame.

Works with your hub

A standard Zigbee 3.0 light: on/off, brightness, colour temperature and colour, groups and scenes. Tested with Zigbee2MQTT, which can also install Outglow updates for you.

The same light

Brightness curves, colours and power-on behaviour copy the original firmware of each bulb, checked level by level against it.

Bluetooth stays

The vendor app still works, and so does the way back to the original firmware. If you don't use Bluetooth, switch it off in Zigbee2MQTT, or keep it on only for a few minutes after power-on.

See what your bulb sees

Neighbours, link quality, retries, radio noise and a black box of the last frames, readable in Zigbee2MQTT. When the network misbehaves, you can find out why.

Open, from the ground up

Written from scratch in Rust, with no vendor code, and tested in an emulator that runs the real firmware images. GPL-3.0-or-later. Not certified by the Connectivity Standards Alliance.

Which bulbs?

Zigbee bulbs and lights sold as EGLO connect.z and AwoX that use the Telink TLSR8258 module: filament bulbs, GU10 and E14 spots, colour bulbs and ceiling lights. Outglow has a firmware image for each of the 21 module versions inside them, and the flasher picks the right one.

How it gets onto a bulb

  1. The first time, over Bluetooth. Open the flasher in Chrome or Edge, pick the bulb, and it installs the right image, the same way the vendor app installs its updates.
  2. After that, over Zigbee. Zigbee2MQTT offers new Outglow versions like any other firmware update.
  3. Changed your mind? The original firmware goes back the same way.
