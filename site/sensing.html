How motion sensing works

Outglow can look for movement by measuring changes in the radio signal between bulbs. The idea is to use the radios already inside the lamps as a motion sensor. Whether it works reliably in a real room still needs to be established.

Experimental. The detector has been tested with simulated signals informed by measurements between real bulbs. Motion sensing itself has not yet run on a physical bulb. The animation below illustrates the design; it is not a recording or a prediction of performance in your room.

1. The bulbs talk to each other

With sensing enabled, each bulb aims to send five short measurement messages per second by default. Sending waits while the radio is busy, and probes do not start during the first minute after startup. They use a separate network identifier so ordinary Zigbee devices ignore them. They still share the same radio channel and add traffic while sensing is on.

A receiving bulb measures how strongly it hears each sender. Each sender-to-receiver path is called a link; a bulb tracks up to ten. You need at least two bulbs with sensing enabled and within radio range.

2. From the signal to motion

The chart follows one simulated path between bulbs. It applies the detector’s scoring rule to the artificial signal used in the animation. Adjust sensitivity to see which changes it treats as motion.

The signal chart uses dBm, a radio-power scale: a more negative value means a weaker signal. A difference in signal strength is measured in dB.

  1. Establish a recent baseline. The detector compares each reading with the middle value of the previous five readings, so one unusual reading does not immediately redefine normal.
  2. Score the change. Small differences contribute little or nothing. Larger differences add more to a score built from recent readings.
  3. Wait for a sustained pattern. The score must stay high enough for a short period before the detector reports movement. At the highest sensitivity, a single sufficiently large change can trigger it.
  4. Adjust sensitivity. A higher setting accepts smaller changes and a shorter pattern. That may detect more movement, but can also produce more false alarms.

3. What the hub sees

The bulb reports occupancy when any tracked path detects motion. It clears that state after a period with no detected motion: thirty seconds by default, shortened to three seconds in this demonstration.

“Occupied” is the name of the Zigbee reading, not proof that someone is still in the room. A person sitting still may stop triggering it. The Outglow converter exposes this reading and its settings in Zigbee2MQTT.

4. Calibration

Every link is noisy in its own way: distance, walls, Wi-Fi on the same frequencies. So every link gets its own sensitivity:

  • Automatic, after a sufficiently sampled day. For a day after sensing is switched on, the bulb watches every link hour by hour. After enough usable samples have been collected, it chooses a sensitivity that would have left the quietest recorded hour free of detections. An interrupted or poorly sampled day may not be enough.
  • By hand. With the room empty, start a calibration: for 60 seconds the bulb records the noise and chooses a sensitivity that leaves a safety margin below the trigger score seen during that recording. Links with too few samples keep their existing setting.
  • Self-tuning, if you want it. Once a day each link may move its sensitivity one step with its noise, never more than two steps from where calibration put it.

Setting a sensitivity yourself turns the automatic calibration off for that bulb.

What it cannot do

  • It detects changing signals, not people directly. Someone sitting still may no longer be detected, and interference or movement elsewhere may cause a false alarm.
  • It needs at least two bulbs with sensing on that hear each other, ideally with the room between them.
  • Radio goes through walls: a link can catch movement in the next room.
  • For 1.5 s after a bulb's light changes (its own or the sender's) the link ignores the signal.
  • Every bulb with sensing on adds radio traffic, targeting five measurement messages per second at the default setting.

Switching it on

Standard Outglow builds include motion sensing, switched off: until you switch it on, it sends no probes and reports nothing. Switch it on with Motion sensing in Zigbee2MQTT (with the Outglow converter) for two or more bulbs in the same room, then allow enough time for automatic calibration or calibrate by hand with the room empty.
