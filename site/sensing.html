How motion sensing works

Bulbs that can hear each other can also tell when someone walks between them. A body absorbs and reflects radio waves at 2.4 GHz, so the signal from one bulb to another wobbles while a person moves through it. Outglow listens for that wobble. No camera, no extra sensor: only the radios the bulbs already have.

Experimental. Motion sensing has been tuned and tested in the Outglow emulator on a radio model built from signal levels measured between real bulbs. It has not yet run on a real bulb.

1. The bulbs talk to each other

With sensing on, every bulb sends a short frame several times a second (5 by default). The frames go to a separate network address of their own (PAN 0x5E55), so the rest of your Zigbee network ignores them; they carry nothing but the sender's light state.

Every bulb measures the signal strength (RSSI) of each of these frames it hears. Each sender it hears is one link; a bulb keeps up to 10 of them. A link exists only between two bulbs that both have sensing on and hear each other.

2. From the signal to motion

Below is one link, from bulb A to bulb B, as the receiving bulb sees it while the person above walks around. The chart computes exactly what the firmware computes.

  1. One sample at a time. The receiver keeps at most 12.5 frames a second per link and ignores frames weaker than −90 dBm.
  2. Compared with the recent past. Each new sample is compared with the median of the five before it. The difference, in dB, divided by a threshold and rounded down, is the sample's weight: 0, 1, 2, or at most 3.
  3. Enough wobble, for long enough. When the weights of the last 16 samples add up to at least M, and keep doing so for a while, the link reports motion. A single disturbed frame is not enough.
  4. Sensitivity sets all three. One number from 1 to 100 picks the threshold, M and how long the rule must hold (10 ms × (100 − sensitivity)): higher is more sensitive. Try the slider above. Each link has its own.

3. Presence

The bulb reports occupied as soon as any of its links sees motion, and unoccupied once none has for the off delay (30 s by default; the demo above uses 3 s). It reports through the standard Zigbee Occupancy Sensing cluster (0x0406), so any Zigbee hub shows it like a motion sensor; the settings live in an Outglow cluster (0xFC5E).

4. Calibration

Every link is noisy in its own way: distance, walls, Wi-Fi on the same frequencies. So every link gets its own sensitivity:

  • Automatic, on the first day. For a day after sensing is switched on, the bulb watches every link hour by hour. Then each link takes the sensitivity its quietest hour allowed: the most sensitive setting at which nothing fired that hour.
  • By hand. With the room empty, start a calibration: for 60 seconds the bulb records the noise and gives each link the most sensitive setting at which the rule never fired, minus a margin.
  • Self-tuning, if you want it. Once a day each link may move its sensitivity one step with its noise, never more than two steps from where calibration put it.

Setting a sensitivity yourself turns the automatic calibration off for that bulb.

What it cannot do

  • Someone sitting still is not seen: it needs movement in the signal. That is why presence switches off after the off delay.
  • It needs at least two bulbs with sensing on that hear each other, ideally with the room between them.
  • Radio goes through walls: a link can catch movement in the next room.
  • For 1.5 s after a bulb's light changes (its own or the sender's) the link ignores the signal.
  • Every bulb with sensing on adds its frames to the channel, 5 a second each, next to your Zigbee traffic.

Switching it on

Every Outglow bulb has motion sensing, switched off: until you switch it on, it sends no probes and reports nothing. Switch it on with Motion sensing in Zigbee2MQTT (with the Outglow converter) for two or more bulbs in the same room, then let the first day calibrate it or calibrate by hand with the room empty.
